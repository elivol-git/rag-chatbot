[Slide 3] Hadar: general information
The Hadar neighborhood was established about 100 years ago, in 1922,  according to the design of the architect Richard Kaufman. It was modeled on Tel Aviv.
The buildings there have extraordinary architecture, but many of them are in a neglected state, and are even in danger of collapse.
Once, the neighborhood was the center of Haifa's commerce and business.

[Slide 4] Landmarks of Hadar
One of the main buildings in lower Hadar is the Talpiot Market, built in 1940 and designed in Bauhaus style , also known as International Style exactly like most buildings in  that area.

[Slide 8] BAUHAUS
“The International Style” or “Bauhaus Style” (German School, very popular in Israel) . This style is characterized by asymmetry, combinations of cubic and rounded volumes, ribbon windows (a series of windows set side by side to form a continuous line horizontally across a façade, shows the interior in a uniform manner), in Haifa you  can also see “thermometer windows”,  balconies, pilotis (pillars), flat roofs and plays of shadow and light. It was based on functionality, emphasized by clean lines and no unnecessary decorations.

[Slide 11] Eclectic style
Eclectic style is an architectural style in which a single piece of work incorporates a mixture of elements from previous historical styles to create something that is new and original. In Israel, eclectic architecture is a blend of local and international styles

[Slide 12] Plans for the future
The Haifa City Council has approved an urban renewal plan for the iconic Talpiot Market building in Haifa and the Syrkin site in the lower Hadar neighborhood in midtown Haifa. The new plan is an important step for urban renewal in the area, which was once Haifa's commercial center. The site has seen ups and downs over the years, and is currently the focus on interest among real estate investors and developers from all over Israel.
